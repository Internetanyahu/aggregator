# Illinois legal review checklist

This checklist is issue-spotting only, not legal advice or a legal opinion. The exact law, regulations, local ordinances, transaction facts, and current agency guidance must be reviewed by an Illinois-licensed real-estate attorney.

Before operating:

- Obtain a written opinion on whether the proposed activity is brokerage, dealing in assignable real-estate contracts, wholesaling, or another regulated activity.
- Confirm the Illinois Real Estate License Act and current IDFPR rules for the intended frequency, compensation, marketing, contract rights, and entity structure.
- Confirm whether an Illinois broker license and sponsoring brokerage are required. Do not assume an LLC, principal-buyer label, one-time contract, or disclosure automatically creates an exemption.
- Have counsel prepare all purchase, assignment, disclosure, cancellation, and earnest-money documents for the actual facts.
- Confirm whether seller consent is required and whether the underlying purchase contract permits assignment.
- Confirm how the equitable interest may be advertised and what must be disclosed to sellers and prospective assignees.
- Confirm all federal, state, and local advertising, consumer-protection, privacy, telemarketing, do-not-call, and fair-housing requirements.
- Use a title company or closing attorney who has approved the exact transaction structure.
- Never claim to own a property when you only hold a contract right. Never claim to have cash or financing that is not available.
- Do not pressure owners, exploit distress, or target protected classes.

Official starting points:

- Illinois DCEO WARN and statutory context: https://dceo.illinois.gov/workforcedevelopment/warn.html
- Illinois Department of Financial and Professional Regulation: https://idfpr.illinois.gov/
- Illinois Real Estate License Act: https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/ilcs3.asp?ActID=1364
